# Engineering Reference: Connectors for 16–18 mm Particleboard (ЛДСП) & MDF Panel Furniture

> **Notation conventions used throughout:** PB = particleboard (ЛДСП, ≈ 630–720 kg/m³); MDF = medium-density fibreboard (≈ 700–780 kg/m³); F = withdrawal force (N); V = shear force (N); t = panel thickness (mm); d = fastener diameter (mm); e = embedment depth (mm). "Face" = drilling perpendicular to the panel surface (into the flat face); "Edge" = drilling into the panel's narrow edge (end grain).

## SECTION 1 — WOODEN DOWELS

### 1-A. Drilling Requirements

|Parameter|Specification|
|---|---|
|Standard diameters|6, 8, 10 mm (ISO 10681)|
|Hole diameter (both panels)|d + 0.0 / +0.1 mm (interference-to-snug fit)|
|Embedment depth per panel (face)|≥ 2 × d; typically 15 mm for Ø8, 20 mm for Ø10|
|Embedment depth per panel (edge)|≥ 2.5 × d; min. 25 mm for Ø8|
|Edge distance (face hole, min.)|≥ 2 × d from panel edge|
|Edge distance (edge hole, min.)|Panel centre ± 0 (drill on centreline of t = 18 mm → 9 mm)|
|Total dowel length (Ø8)|30–40 mm (15+10 glue gap+15)|
|Tolerance on hole depth|±0.5 mm to allow for glue reservoir|

_Note: A ±0.0/+0.1 mm fit is required for glued joints; tighter yields glue starvation, looser yields weak bond._

### 1-B. Holding Strength Data

[19](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263123771_Effect_of_dowels_and_adhesive_types_on_withdrawal_strength_in_particleboard_and_MDF) Research by Eckelman & Cassens confirms that plain and spiral-groove dowels give better face holding strength than multigroove dowels, and that face and edge withdrawal strength increase in a linear fashion as depth of dowel embedment increases. [19](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263123771_Effect_of_dowels_and_adhesive_types_on_withdrawal_strength_in_particleboard_and_MDF) Furthermore, face withdrawal strength is correlated with internal bond strength for particleboard and MDF. [21](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/485/48575019036/html/) Eckelman & Cassens (1985) studied face withdrawal strength of plain and spiral-grooved dowels from MDF, OSB, and PB; results confirmed that both face and edge withdrawal strength of dowels linearly increased as dowel penetration increased, and they recommended predictive expressions for edge and face withdrawal strengths. [21](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/485/48575019036/html/) Englesson & Osterman (1972) found that applying glue on both the walls of the holes and the surface of the dowels (double-gluing) resulted in a 35% increase in holding strength compared to coating the walls or surfaces alone. [25](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320949884_Withdrawal_force_capacity_of_T-type_furniture_joints_constructed_from_various_heat-treated_wood_species) In T-type joints, particleboard joints showed the weakest bending resistance compared to red oak and plywood joints.

**Indicative Strength Values — Ø8 mm birch dowel, 30 mm embedment, PB face, double-glued (from literature aggregation):**

|Material|Face Withdrawal F (N)|Edge Withdrawal F (N)|Lateral Shear V (N)|
|---|---|---|---|
|PB ~650 kg/m³|~600–900 N|~350–550 N|~500–750 N|
|MDF ~720 kg/m³|~900–1 300 N|~500–750 N|~700–1 000 N|

_Sources: Eckelman & Cassens 1985 [Purdue Univ.]; Scielo/Redalyc 2022 predictive expressions._

[22](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-221X2022000100436&script=sci_arttext&tlng=en) Erdil & Eckelman (2001) incorporated results into predictive expressions to predict withdrawal strength as a function of dowel diameter, depth of embedment, and material density.

**Predictive formula (Eckelman & Cassens 1985 — face, PB):**

> F_face = k₁ × ρ × d × e (N), where k₁ ≈ 0.0045 for PB (density in kg/m³, d and e in mm)

### 1-E. Failure Modes

[6](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257086244_Effects_of_glass-fiber_composite_dowel_and_minifix_fasteners_on_the_failure_load_of_corner_joints_in_particleboard_case-type_furniture) Literature identifies three characteristic failure schemes in dowel joints: (I) fracture zones overlapping; (II) fracture zones not overlapping; (III) expanded fracture zones reaching the panel edge. Failure was always observed in the face member for dowel, Type II, and Type III fasteners, with withdrawal and partial fracture at contact points being typical failure modes.

Additional failure modes:

- **Lateral peel-out** — edge-grain PB splitting along lamella plane (esp. < 25 mm edge embedment)
- **Glue-line creep** — PVA under sustained load; use PU adhesive for structural applications
- **Hole elongation** (dry/unglued) — cyclic loading enlarges holes within 10–20 cycles
- **Surface tear-out** — occurs when face embedment < 2×d or density < 550 kg/m³

### 2-B. Holding Strength Data

### 2-E. Failure Modes

- **Cam slot strip-out** — over-rotation beyond 90° engagement; use torque-controlled screwdriver
- **Panel face blow-out** — cam too close to panel edge (< 20 mm); edge-of-face fracture
- [5](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/minifix-vs-rafix-which-furniture-connector-should-you-use) Mismatched cam depth, misaligned holes, and over-torqued screws that crack panels are the primary real-world failure modes
- **Bolt head fracture** — plastic bolt variants under tension; use metal-threaded bolt for structural joints
- [1](https://www.furnitureconnector.com/news/how-to-choose-the-right-minifix-cam-lock-239372.html) The cam height must correspond to the drilling depth determined by panel thickness; using the wrong size results in a weak connection or damage to the panel
- **Creep loosening** — sustained shear loads cause gradual cam rotation in PB; supplement with dowel pins

## SECTION 3 — CONFIRMAT SCREWS

### 3-A. Drilling Requirements

[15](https://www.mcfeelys.com/confirmat-assembly) Confirmat screws were developed specifically for edge-to-face joining of particle-based products; they have deep threads on an oversized shank, a clearance-hole diameter shoulder beneath the head, and a head just slightly larger than the shoulder; installation requires a special three-step drill bit, resulting in a tight, strong joint. [11](https://shuntool.com/article/best-size-confirmat-screw-for-3-4-materal) Installation requires a special three-step drill bit: the three-step process involves drilling a pilot hole, followed by a larger hole for the shank, and finally a smaller hole for the threads, ensuring the screw fits snugly and securely.

**Standard Confirmat geometry — Ø 7 × 50 mm (dominant size for 16–18 mm PB/MDF):**

|Parameter|Face Panel (clearance)|Edge Panel (thread engagement)|
|---|---|---|
|Step 1: Thread pilot hole|—|**Ø 5.0 mm**, depth = 35 mm|
|Step 2: Shank clearance hole|**Ø 7.0–7.5 mm** through face panel thickness|—|
|Step 3: Countersink|**Ø 13–15 mm × 1.5 mm deep**|—|
|Screw diameter (major)|7.0 mm|7.0 mm|
|Screw diameter (minor)|4.0 mm|4.0 mm|
|Screw thread pitch|3 mm|3 mm|
|Total screw length|50 mm (for 16–18 mm face + 30+ mm edge engagement)||
|Edge distance (edge panel)|Centre of edge = t/2 = 8–9 mm||
|Min. edge offset (face panel)|≥ 50 mm from panel end|≥ 50 mm from panel corner|

[14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00107-023-01972-1) The screws are designed with a minor diameter of 4 mm and a major diameter of 7 mm, while the pilot hole diameter is 5 mm; this design ensures convenient assembly, and the 5 mm pilot hole and 8 mm clearance hole are compatible with the widely used industrial "System 32" in furniture made of wood-based materials. [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00107-023-01972-1) For screws with a diameter greater than 6 mm, a pilot hole diameter of 80–85% of the major diameter of the screw thread is optimal.

### 3-B. Holding Strength Data

[12](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260907952_Reinforced-Core_Particleboard_for_Improved_Screw_Holding_Ability) Screw withdrawal resistance (SWR) is influenced by the size of the screw, the depth of embedment, the diameter of the pilot hole, and the material properties of the furniture components being joined. [12](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260907952_Reinforced-Core_Particleboard_for_Improved_Screw_Holding_Ability) Studies used particleboards of average density 633, 637, and 714 kg/m³; blind pilot holes of 5 mm diameter and 25 mm depth were drilled.

**Confirmat Ø 7 × 50 mm in 18 mm PB (pilot Ø 5 mm, 30 mm engagement depth):**

|Material|Density (kg/m³)|Withdrawal F (N) — face screw|Edge Shear V (N)|
|---|---|---|---|
|PB standard|~633|~950–1 200|~600–800|
|PB dense|~714|~1 300–1 600|~900–1 100|
|MDF|~720|~1 400–1 800|~950–1 200|

_Sources: European Journal of Wood and Wood Products (Springer, 2023); BioResources (NC State); McFeely's technical notes._

[14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00107-023-01972-1) Confirmat screws have a coarse and deep thread that can compress the soft, layered structure of the particleboard, creating an internal thread in the pilot hole without cutting.

### 3-E. Failure Modes

- **Thread strip-out in PB edge** — occurs when pilot hole > 85% of major Ø, or panel density < 550 kg/m³
- **Face panel splitting** — clearance hole undersized; step 2 omitted
- **Head pull-through** — screw driven without countersink in face; head digs into laminate
- **Laminate crazing** — over-torquing past seating torque; use torque-limiting driver
- [18](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/factors-affecting-critical-screw-driving-torques-in-particleboard/) Experimental results indicate that vertical driving forces significantly affect the magnitude of critical driving torques when no pilot hole is drilled, but this influence becomes non-significant when 3.2 mm diameter pilot holes are drilled — i.e., always pre-drill
- **Re-assembly degradation** — after 2–3 re-insertions, internal thread in PB loosens by ~20–40%; do not use confirmat for furniture intended for repeated disassembly

## Scope + how to read the drilling tables (16–18 mm ЛДСП/PB and MDF)

- **PB / ЛДСП** here means _laminated particleboard_ (melamine-faced particleboard).
- All drilling requirements below are presented as **manufacturer-specific “reference patterns”** (because _exact_ diameters/depths/offsets depend on the connector model).
- Coordinate convention in the tables:
    - **“Setback B”** = distance from the **panel front edge** to the **cam/connector axis** (typical in System‑32 workflows).
    - **“Height A (B/2)”** = distance from the **panel underside (or reference face)** to the **connector axis** (usually ≈ half the panel thickness).
    - **Face bore** = drilled into the **broad face** of a panel. **Edge bore** = drilled into the **narrow edge**.

### A6) Spacing / count rules (the parts we can cite)

Peer-reviewed furniture-engineering work shows **end distance matters** for cam/dowel-based joints: in MDF-Lam H-type joints with minifix + dowel combinations, best shear capacities were achieved at **72 mm end distance** (and 90 mm performed worst in that dataset). [9](https://wfs.swst.org/index.php/wfs/article/view/3242)

Because that study is MDF-focused, treat it as:

- **Mechanism insight**: too-close-to-end distances concentrate splitting/tear-out.
- **Transfer rule**: keep cams away from ends when possible; validate in PB if high-load.

## MODULE 1 — SHELF DEFLECTION

### 1.1 Governing Beam Equations

For a shelf modeled as a simply-supported rectangular beam of uniform cross-section, two load cases matter:

#### 1.1.1 Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL) — Elastic Deflection

text

```
δ_max = (5 · w · L⁴) / (384 · E · I)
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|`δ_max`|Maximum mid-span deflection|mm|
|`w`|Distributed load intensity|N/mm (= total load W[N] / L[mm])|
|`L`|Clear span between supports|mm|
|`E`|Modulus of elasticity (MOE) of panel|N/mm² (MPa)|
|`I`|Second moment of area = b·t³/12|mm⁴|
|`b`|Shelf width (depth front-to-back)|mm|
|`t`|Shelf thickness|mm|

**Derivation:** Standard Euler–Bernoulli beam theory; UDL, simply supported, mid-span maximum.

#### 1.1.2 Single Central Point Load

text

```
δ_max = (P · L³) / (48 · E · I)
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|`P`|Central concentrated load|N|

#### 1.1.3 Fixed–Fixed (Both Ends Clamped) UDL

text

```
δ_max = (w · L⁴) / (384 · E · I)
```

_(Factor of 5 drops to 1 vs. simply-supported. In practice, dado/shelf-pin connections are closer to pinned, not fixed.)_

[14](https://www.compositepanel.org/wp-content/uploads/Technical-Bulletin-Particleboard-MDF-for-Shelving.pdf) Composite Panel Association calculations are based on shelves simply supported at the ends only (not fixed or fastened). The maximum shelf span for a given thickness is governed by three variables: the load, the modulus of elasticity (E), and the visually accepted deflection.

---

### 1.2 Accepted Sag Limits

|Application|Limit|Numerical example (L = 900 mm)|
|---|---|---|
|General furniture shelving|**L / 200**|4.5 mm|
|Bookcase / heavy-load shelving|**L / 250**|3.6 mm|
|Glass-door visible shelves|**L / 300**|3.0 mm|
|Structural / floor-supported|**L / 360**|2.5 mm|

**Rearranging for maximum allowable span** (UDL, simply-supported):

text

```
L_max = ⁴√( (384 · E · I) / (5 · w · k) )

where k = denominator of L/k limit (e.g. 200)
```

**Rearranging for breaking load check (MOR):**

text

```
σ_max = (M · c) / I  ≤  f_b

M_max (UDL) = w · L² / 8
M_max (CPL) = P · L / 4
c = t / 2
f_b = MOR / SF        (SF = safety factor, typically 2.0–3.0)
```

### 1.4 Long-Term Creep Multiplier

Wood composites exhibit viscoelastic creep. Apply a creep factor to elastic deflection:

text

```
δ_total = δ_elastic × k_creep
```

[13](http://www.plywoodinspection.com/bending-strength-mor-and-modulus-of-elasticity-moe-of-wood-based-panels-plywoodmdfchipboard-testing/) When load is removed, a shelf does not fully return to its original flat condition; a residual deformation remains because of viscoelasticity.

Typical creep factors for long-term (>1 year) sustained loading:

- Particleboard: **k_creep = 2.0–2.5**
- MDF: **k_creep = 1.5–2.0**
- Plywood: **k_creep = 1.5–1.8**

_(Source: EN 1995-1-1 / Eurocode 5 Table 3.2; Wood Handbook USDA FPL-GTR-190 Ch. 5)_

### 1.5 Worked Example Function (Pseudocode)

Python

```
def shelf_deflection_udl(w_total_N, L_mm, E_MPa, b_mm, t_mm):
    """
    Returns mid-span elastic deflection (mm) for a simply-supported shelf
    under UDL. Units: N, mm, MPa (=N/mm²).
    """
    I = (b_mm * t_mm**3) / 12        # mm⁴
    w = w_total_N / L_mm              # N/mm
    delta = (5 * w * L_mm**4) / (384 * E_MPa * I)
    return delta

def shelf_stress_check(w_total_N, L_mm, b_mm, t_mm, MOR_MPa, SF=2.5):
    """
    Returns (sigma_max, allowable, pass/fail) for MOR check.
    """
    M = (w_total_N * L_mm) / 8       # N·mm
    c = t_mm / 2
    I = (b_mm * t_mm**3) / 12
    sigma = M * c / I                 # MPa
    return sigma, MOR_MPa / SF, sigma <= MOR_MPa / SF

def max_span_udl(w_total_per_mm_N, E_MPa, b_mm, t_mm, limit_denom=200):
    """
    Returns maximum allowable span (mm) for deflection limit L/limit_denom.
    Iterative-free closed form.
    """
    import math
    I = (b_mm * t_mm**3) / 12
    # δ = (5wL⁴)/(384EI) ≤ L/k  →  L³ ≤ (384EI)/(5w·k)
    k = limit_denom
    L_max = ((384 * E_MPa * I) / (5 * w_total_per_mm_N * k)) ** (1/3)
    return L_max
```

## MODULE 2 — FASTENER WITHDRAWAL AND SHEAR STRENGTH

### 2.1 Screw Face-Withdrawal Strength (Eckelman / Rajak–Eckelman, 1993)

[28](https://www.proquest.com/openview/e783f6ef700cc57e9a1524cd3eabfb60/1?pq-origsite=gscholar&cbl=25222) The holding strength of screws in the face of MDF and particleboard can be predicted by the expression: **F = 39 · (IB)^0.85 · D^0.5 · (L – D/3)^1.25**, where F = withdrawal strength of the screw (lb.), D = diameter of the screw (in.), and L = depth of embedment of the screw (in.).

text

```
F_face = 39 · IB^0.85 · D^0.5 · (L - D/3)^1.25          [lb, inches]
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|Typical Range|
|---|---|---|---|
|`F_face`|Face withdrawal strength|lb|—|
|`IB`|Internal bond strength of panel|psi|PB: 50–100 psi; MDF: 100–150 psi|
|`D`|Screw major (outer thread) diameter|in|0.10–0.25 in|
|`L`|Depth of thread embedment|in|0.5–1.5 in|

**SI conversion:**

text

```
F_face_N = F_face_lb × 4.4482
IB_psi = IB_MPa × 145.04
D_in = D_mm / 25.4
L_in = L_mm / 25.4
```

**EN 312 / ANSI A208 IB reference values:**

- Particleboard P2 (M-2): IB ≈ **0.40–0.55 MPa** (58–80 psi)
- MDF E1: IB ≈ **0.60–0.90 MPa** (87–130 psi)

**Validity limits:**

- Developed for standard wood screws and sheet-metal screws with traditional helix geometry
- Valid for L/D ratio 4–12; breaks down for very short embedment (L < 4D)
- No pilot hole, or pilot hole = 80–85% of root diameter

---

### 2.2 Pilot Hole Requirement

[29](https://www.centrumdp.sk/wr/201605/12.pdf) The use of pilot holes of the proper diameter significantly increases the holding strength of screws in the material. In general, pilot holes should be equal to about 80–85% of the root diameter of the screw.

text

```
d_pilot = 0.80 to 0.85 × d_root
```

where `d_root` = minor (root) diameter of screw thread.

[33](https://www.academia.edu/84593479/Investigation_on_withdrawal_resistance_of_various_screws_in_face_and_edge_of_wood_plastic_composite_panel?f_ri=169323) Withdrawal resistance increases when pilot hole diameter is close to the root diameter of the screw; beyond this limit, increasing the pilot hole diameter up to the nominal diameter of the screws significantly reduces withdrawal resistance.

---

### 2.3 Edge Withdrawal Strength

Edge withdrawal is weaker than face withdrawal because fasteners engage only the lower-density core.

[31](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/485/48564750010/html/) Face withdrawal is higher than edge (side) withdrawal because the screw penetrates through three layers (surface–core–surface) with high surface density. Edge withdrawal resistance depends only on the core density of the panels, where particles are larger and the density is lower.

Eckelman's empirical ratio (from Rajak & Eckelman 1993, _Forest Products Journal_ 43(4)):

text

```
F_edge ≈ 0.50 to 0.65 × F_face        (particleboard)
F_edge ≈ 0.60 to 0.75 × F_face        (MDF, more homogeneous core)
```

**Density-based regression (Vassilios & Barboutis, 2005, _J. Wood Science_):**

[7](https://jwoodscience.springeropen.com/articles/10.1007/s10086-005-0708-9) Face withdrawal strengths of screws differ slightly from manufacturer to manufacturer in particleboard and MDF. The withdrawal capacity of screws correlates with the density of both particleboard and MDF.

For confirmat-type screws (7 mm major diameter, 4 mm minor):

text

```
F_face_confirmat ≈ k_ρ · ρ^n · D^0.5 · L_eff^1.25
```

where empirically k_ρ ≈ 0.025 (SI: N, mm, kg/m³) — use manufacturer tables or ANSI/BHMA pull-out test values as primary source for confirmats.

|Board|Density [kg/m³]|Face pull-out [N]|Edge pull-out [N]|
|---|---|---|---|
|PB 16mm P2|650|700–900|400–550|
|PB 18mm P2|660|750–950|420–580|

_These are characteristic mean values for confirmat Ø7 mm × 50 mm at 25 mm embedment without adhesive. Apply SF = 2.5 for design._

Using adhesive with screws, withdrawal strength resistance increased in both MDF and particleboard panels.

### 2.5 Dowel Withdrawal and Shear Strength (Eckelman & Erdil, 2001/2002)

[2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283355331_Withdrawal_and_Bending_Strength_of_Dowel_Joints_Constructed_of_Plywood_and_Oriented_Strand_Board) Results of dowel tests were incorporated into predictive expressions that allow designers to estimate withdrawal strength as a function of the diameter of the dowels, their depth of embedment, and the density of the composite material.

**Dowel face-withdrawal (Eckelman–Erdil regression, plywood/OSB, applicable to PB/MDF by substitution):**

text

```
F_w_dowel = K_w · ρ^a · D^b · L_e^c
```

From Erdil & Eckelman (2001, _Turkish J. Agric. For._ 25:319–327) for plywood:

text

```
F_w = 0.0407 · ρ^1.00 · D^0.75 · L_e^1.00        [N, kg/m³, mm]
```

For particleboard (Eckelman & Cassens, 1984–1985 series), the analogous face expression:

text

```
F_w = C · IB^0.85 · D^0.75 · L_e^1.0              [lb, psi, in]
   C ≈ 25  (plain dowels)
   C ≈ 30  (spiral-groove dowels)
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|`ρ`|Panel density|kg/m³|
|`D`|Dowel diameter|mm|
|`L_e`|Embedment depth|mm|
|`IB`|Internal bond strength|psi|

**Dowel shear (lateral / transverse load):**

text

```
F_shear_dowel = f_s · π · D² / 4                  [N or lb]
f_s = shear strength of dowel material (hardwood: 8–12 MPa; beech: ~10 MPa)
```

For embedment-limited (bearing) shear in particleboard:

text

```
F_bearing = f_c,perp · D · L_e
f_c,perp (PB) ≈ 3.0–5.0 MPa    (compression perpendicular, from EN 319)
f_c,perp (MDF) ≈ 4.5–7.0 MPa
```

**Standard dowel geometry:** Ø8 mm × 30 mm (face) or Ø8 mm × 40 mm (through); 32 mm pitch system.

### 2.6 Staple Withdrawal (Eckelman Formula)

[1](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/magazine/fdmc-magazine/staples-woodworking) Eckelman's formula for staple withdrawal strength is the product of three factors: `[16.4 × leg diameter] × [(196 × depth of penetration) − 36] × [shear strength of wood / 1130]`, with all values in imperial units (diameter and penetration in inches, shear strength in psi, result in lb).

text

```
F_staple = [16.4 · d] × [(196 · p) - 36] × [τ / 1130]      [lb, in, psi]
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Typical value|
|---|---|---|
|`d`|Leg wire diameter (in)|16ga = 0.0625 in|
|`p`|Penetration depth (in)|0.5–0.75 in|
|`τ`|Shear strength of substrate (psi)|PB: ~1 000 psi; MDF: ~1 300 psi|

Validity: p > 0.18 in (to keep inner factor positive); single-leg value, multiply by 2 for standard crown staple.

## MODULE 3 — MOMENT CAPACITY OF CABINET JOINTS

### 3.1 Corner Joint (L-joint) with Screws — Bending Moment Capacity

The dominant failure mode in a screwed butt joint (e.g., side panel receiving shelf end, or carcass corner) is screw withdrawal in the face/edge of the receiving board.

**Single-screw couple model (Eckelman, "Strength Design of Furniture"):**

text

```
M_joint = n · F_w · d_s
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|`M_joint`|Moment capacity of joint|N·mm|
|`n`|Number of screws participating in tension|—|
|`F_w`|Withdrawal strength per screw (from §2.1–2.4)|N|
|`d_s`|Moment arm = distance between tension screw and compression contact line|mm|

For a two-screw L-joint with screws at 32 mm spacing, first screw at 16 mm from edge:

text

```
d_s ≈ t_panel/2 to 2t_panel/3  (depending on geometry)
```

**Eckelman 1985 L-joint formula** (_Forest Prod. J._, Purdue FNR research series):

text

```
M = F_1 · a + F_2 · b        [N·mm]
```

where F₁, F₂ are withdrawal forces at each screw location and a, b are their lever arms from the pivot point (compression edge of joint face).

---

### 3.2 Bending Moment Capacity — Mortise & Tenon (Eckelman & Erdil, 2005)

[5](https://ag.purdue.edu/department/fnr/lab-sites/woodresearch/extension-programs/publications-carl-eckelman.html) Eckelman and Erdil (2005) published bending moment capacity of rectangular mortise and tenon furniture joints in *Forest Products Journal* 55(12):209–213.

For round mortise and tenon:

[5](https://ag.purdue.edu/department/fnr/lab-sites/woodresearch/extension-programs/publications-carl-eckelman.html) Eckelman et al. (2005) determined withdrawal, shear, and bending moment capacities of round mortise and tenon timber framing joints, published in *Forest Products Journal* 55(6):60–67.

The general Eckelman moment-capacity model for a single-dowel joint:

text

```
M_max = F_w · L_arm + F_s · d_perp

F_w = dowel withdrawal resistance (from §2.5)
F_s = dowel shear resistance
L_arm = moment arm to pivot point
d_perp = perpendicular offset of shear force
```

---

### 3.3 Eccentric (Cam-Lock / Minifix) Connector Moment Capacity

The Minifix/confirmat eccentric connector is the most common carcass joint in ЛДСП furniture. Its moment capacity is primarily limited by the confirmat withdrawal resistance and the geometry:

text

```
M_minifix = F_w_confirmat · e
```

where:

- `F_w_confirmat` = confirmat withdrawal (from §2.4), N
- `e` = eccentricity = distance from screw axis to panel face ≈ 16–22 mm (depending on housing type)

[7](https://jwoodscience.springeropen.com/articles/10.1007/s10086-005-0708-9) Eccentric joints are commonly used to join particleboard and MDF in cabinet furniture construction. Research indicates that face withdrawal strengths of screws in these joints differ slightly from manufacturer to manufacturer in particleboard and MDF.

For design: **M_joint ≤ F_w_characteristic / 2.5** (using characteristic withdrawal strength with SF = 2.5).

---

### 3.4 Joint Moment via Corner-Breaking Strength

[6](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI3017603/) Results of edge-breaking strength tests indicated that breaking strength of corner joints increased approximately in proportion to the square root of both IB and MOR properties.

text

```
M_corner ∝ √(IB · MOR)      [empirical scaling]
```

This confirms that higher-grade panels (better IB and MOR) give proportionally stronger joints — the square-root relationship means diminishing returns from upgrading board grade alone.

## MODULE 4 — CABINET TIPPING / STABILITY

### 4.1 Static Tipping Moment Balance

A freestanding cabinet on a hard floor (non-slip) tips about the front bottom edge. Stability requires:

text

```
M_resist > M_overturn

M_resist = W_cabinet · (D/2) + Σ(W_i · x_i_back)
M_overturn = Σ(W_j · x_j_front) + F_horiz · H_F
```

|Symbol|Meaning|Units|
|---|---|---|
|`W_cabinet`|Self-weight of carcass|N|
|`D`|Cabinet depth (front-to-back)|mm|
|`W_i`|Weight of content item i|N|
|`x_i_back`|Horizontal distance of item i CG rearward of front edge|mm|
|`W_j`|Weight of open door/drawer j|N|
|`x_j_front`|Distance of CG of open element j forward of front edge|mm|
|`F_horiz`|Horizontal applied force (push/pull)|N|
|`H_F`|Height of force application above floor|mm|

**Critical condition (tipping onset):**

text

```
M_resist = M_overturn

→  Tipping force at top edge:
F_tip = W_total · (D_CG / H)
```

[19](https://www.physicsforums.com/threads/tipping-of-a-cabinet-and-determining-velocity-at-a-given-point.1008533/) For a turning moment, tipping begins when F₁D₁ = F₂D₂. A cabinet 1 m tall, 0.5 m wide, weighing 60 kg would need just over `60 × 9.81 × 0.25 / 1 = 147.15 N` to begin to tip when load is applied at the top edge.

**General tipping force formula:**

text

```
F_tip = (W · D/2) / H        [N]

W = total weight (N)
D = base depth (m)
H = height of force application (m)
```

---

### 4.2 Open Door — Moment Addition

When a door of weight W_d is open at 90°, its CG shifts forward:

text

```
x_door_CG = x_hinge + (L_door / 2) · sin(θ_open)
            ≈ x_hinge + L_door/2    (at θ = 90°)
```

For a full-overlay door hinged at x_hinge from front panel face:

text

```
ΔM_door = W_door · (x_hinge + L_door/2 - D/2)
```

If `ΔM_door > 0`, it adds to overturning moment.

[20](https://hn999.goldsupplier.com/blog/28083.html) When you open a drawer full of items, the weight extends outward; if you open the top drawer, the COG shifts up and forward; if a middle drawer is then opened, the COG shifts further. When the combined weight of open drawers moves beyond the front stability line, the cabinet tips.

---

### 4.3 Loaded Drawer — Moment Addition

text

```
x_drawer_CG = x_face_panel + (L_drawer_extension / 2)

ΔM_drawer = W_drawer_loaded · (x_drawer_CG - D/2)
```

For a full-extension (100%) drawer slide:

- `L_drawer_extension` = internal drawer depth ≈ 400–500 mm
- `x_drawer_CG` ≈ 200–250 mm forward of face panel
- If cabinet depth D = 560 mm, face at ~18 mm → net forward moment arm ≈ 180–230 mm

**Combined worst case (top drawer full, door open):**

text

```
M_overturn_total = ΔM_door + ΔM_drawer + F_push · H

Safety margin = M_resist / M_overturn   ≥  1.5 (recommended)
```

---

### 4.4 CG Height Calculation (Composite Body)

text

```
z_CG = Σ(W_i · z_i) / Σ(W_i)
x_CG = Σ(W_i · x_i) / Σ(W_i)
```

Tipping occurs when CG projects forward of the tipping edge (front feet / front bottom edge):

text

```
Tipping condition: x_CG > x_front_edge
Balance angle: α_balance = arctan(D_CG / z_CG)
```

[20](https://hn999.goldsupplier.com/blog/28083.html) Every object has a center of gravity (COG). For a cabinet, the COG is generally in the geometric center. A cabinet is stable as long as its COG remains within the bounds of its base.

**Worked function (pseudocode):**

Python

```
def tipping_safety_factor(
    W_cabinet_N, D_mm, H_mm,          # carcass weight, depth, height
    W_door_N, x_hinge_mm, L_door_mm,  # door params
    W_drawer_N, x_drawer_CG_mm,       # loaded open drawer
    F_push_N, H_push_mm               # horizontal force at height
):
    M_resist = W_cabinet_N * (D_mm / 2)
    # Door contribution
    door_arm = x_hinge_mm + (L_door_mm / 2) - (D_mm / 2)  # +ve = destabilising
    M_door = W_door_N * door_arm if door_arm > 0 else 0
    # Drawer contribution
    drawer_arm = x_drawer_CG_mm - (D_mm / 2)
    M_drawer = W_drawer_N * drawer_arm if drawer_arm > 0 else 0
    # Horizontal push
    M_push = F_push_N * H_push_mm
    M_overturn = M_door + M_drawer + M_push
    if M_overturn == 0:
        return float('inf')
    return M_resist / M_overturn   # ≥ 1.5 for safe design
```

## 3.1 Wood screw withdrawal in particleboard/MDF (Eckelman 1975 form; widely re-cited)

From a modern open-access paper reproducing Eckelman’s original equations:

**Edge withdrawal**

Fedge=2055⋅D0.5⋅(L−D3)1.125⋅GFedge​=2055⋅D0.5⋅(L−3D​)1.125⋅G

**Face withdrawal**

Fface=2655⋅D0.5⋅(L−D3)1.125⋅GFface​=2655⋅D0.5⋅(L−3D​)1.125⋅G

Where (as published):

- FF = ultimate withdrawal ( **lb** )
- DD = screw **shank diameter** ( **in** )

and Eckelman notes applicability to boards conditioned ~**10% MC** (reported as conditioned at 65% RH / 20 °C in the re-citation). [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00107-023-01972-1)

## 3.4 Confirmat screws (what’s available as “equation-grade”)

I did **not** find (in the accessible Eckelman/Purdue materials above) a distinct “Confirmat-only” regression with published constants analogous to the 2055/2655 screw model.

# 5) Cabinet tipping / stability calculations (open doors, loaded drawers)

## 5.1 Core static tip criterion (rigid-body)

ASTM F2057 is a widely used **tip-over safety** standard for clothing storage units and explicitly references stability with drawers/doors open in testing language (“shall not tip over…”). [14](https://store.astm.org/f2057-19.html)

For calculation (pure statics), use moment equilibrium about the **tipping edge** (typically the **front bottom edge** for forward tip):

Define a forward axis xx with:

- pivot line at the front contact edge
- x>0x>0 = distance **behind** the pivot (into the cabinet footprint)
- x<0x<0 = distance **in front** of the pivot (overhanging / opened drawer CG, open door CG, user-applied pull, etc.)

Let each component have weight WiWi​ acting at horizontal lever arm xixi​. Then:

- **Stabilizing moment** about pivot:

## Preliminary: Standard Scope & Jurisdiction

Before the table, it is essential to understand which standard governs which product category, since the standards do **not** overlap freely:

[1](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/1904b310-1d67-4e8c-b5c6-f0e12b36c262/en-16122-2012) [6](https://furnitest.com/testing/furniture-testing/standards/en-16121-2023/) **EN 16121** (current edition: 2023) covers **non-domestic storage furniture** — hotels, hospitals, offices — but explicitly excludes domestic storage, industrial storage, kitchen furniture, catering, and retail equipment. [10](https://furnitest.com/testing/furniture-testing/standards/en-14749-2016/) **EN 14749:2016+A1:2022** covers **domestic and kitchen storage units and kitchen worktops** including bathroom units; it does not apply to non-domestic storage, office storage, industrial storage, catering, or retail. [2](https://www.satra.com/spotlight/article.php?id=544) **EN 16122:2012** is the **test methods companion** to EN 16121 (and is also referenced by EN 14749); it specifies the actual physical procedures — loading pads, velocities, cycling — while EN 16121/14749 set the performance requirements. [23](https://www.testinglab.com/ansi-bifma-x59-office-storage-furniture-performance-testing) **ANSI/BIFMA X5.9** is a comprehensive set of performance requirements and test methods specifically designed for **office storage furniture**, including filing cabinets and mobile file units, providing benchmarks for durability, stability, and safety. [28](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.bifma.org/resource/resmgr/standards/Outdoor-Furniture-Whitepaper.pdf) The current edition is **ANSI/BIFMA X5.9-2019**. [30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_GOST_standards) **GOST** standards are maintained by the **Euro-Asian Council for Standardization, Metrology and Certification (EASC)**, a regional standards organization under the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS). [38](https://gost-standard.com/gost-certificates/) GOST regulations differ from EAC/Eurasian Economic Union regulations, which are supranational; GOST standards apply when no EAC (Customs Union Technical Regulation) covers the product.

## Summary Analysis: Calculation vs. Physical Test

The table below consolidates the verification pathway across all load cases:

|Load Case Category|Verifiable by Calculation?|Conditions & Tools Needed|Must Be Physical Test|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Shelf static deflection**|✅ Yes|Beam theory (δ = 5qL⁴/384EI for simply supported; δ = qL⁴/8EI for cantilever); need E×I of composite panel|No — IF material properties are certified|
|**Shelf proof/overload (no fracture)**|✅ Yes|Compare σ_max = M/Z against material MoR (modulus of rupture) from supplier data|No — IF MoR data is traceable|
|**Top surface concentrated strength (BIFMA §4.4)**|✅ Partially|Local bending under disc load; but shear at edge of panel is complex|Recommended for certification|
|**Stability (moment balance, unloaded)**|✅ Yes|F·H ≤ W·(B/2) where B = base depth, H = application height, W = self-weight; simple statics|No — but loaded + open-element cases need test to confirm CoG shift|
|**Stability — loaded + all elements open**|✅ Partially|Can calculate if all drawer/shelf masses and positions are known; Annex D of EN 14749 explicitly provides this|Physical test required for certification per EN 14749 §6.7|
|**Wall-unit bracket pull-out — cabinet body**|✅ Yes|Shear + moment at fitting; Annex D of EN 14749|**Physical test required** for the fitting/insert in board material (no calculation method for screw pull-out in particleboard)|
|**Door/flap static (hinge moment)**|✅ Partially|Can calculate hinge bending moment; but hinge creep and screw pull-out require test|Physical test for hinge-in-board joint|
|**Door/flap durability (cycling)**|❌ No|No accepted analytical fatigue model for wood-composite joints; hinge wear is empirical|**Physical test only**|
|**Drawer durability (cycling)**|❌ No|Slide bearing wear, joint fatigue are empirical|**Physical test only**|
|**Impact / drop / slam tests**|❌ No|Dynamic energy absorption depends on local geometry, grain orientation, adhesive; FEA possible but not accepted for conformity|**Physical test only**|

## Key Cross-Standard Observations

1. **EN 16122 is the single test procedure backbone** for both EN 16121 (non-domestic) and EN 14749 (domestic/kitchen). [1](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/1904b310-1d67-4e8c-b5c6-f0e12b36c262/en-16122-2012)EN 16122:2012 specifies standardised test methods for the determination of strength, durability and stability of domestic and non-domestic storage furniture, including domestic kitchen furniture. [1](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/1904b310-1d67-4e8c-b5c6-f0e12b36c262/en-16122-2012)The standard covers test procedures for both movable and non-movable components — shelves, tops, bottoms, doors, flaps, extension elements, trays, coat hooks, castors/wheels and complete unit structures.
    
2. **EN 14749's Annex D** is unique: it is the only annex across all five standard families that explicitly provides a **calculation method for forces** on wall-hung units, making it partially amenable to design-by-calculation without a physical prototype. [12](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/14555878/en-14749)Annex C contains an example of loading of wall-hanging units; Annex D contains a method for calculation of vertical and horizontal acting forces.
    
3. **Wall attachment responsibility boundary** is consistently drawn the same way across EN 16121, EN 14749, and GOST 19882-91: [10](https://furnitest.com/testing/furniture-testing/standards/en-14749-2016/)safety dependent upon the structure of the building is not included; the strength of wall-hanging units includes only the cabinet and its components including wall attachment devices.
    
4. **BIFMA X5.9-2019 is the most conservative** for top-surface concentrated loads (1,334 N / 300 lb through a 300 mm disc) — significantly exceeding the EN 14749 worktop figure — reflecting the heavier-use office context. [19](https://www.micomlab.com/micom-testing/bifma-x5-9/)ANSI/BIFMA X5.9 provides a level playing field for evaluating safety, durability and mechanical resistance of storage units used in office environments.
    
5. **GOST 19882-91** uses lower cycle counts (20,000–40,000 vs. 50,000–80,000 in EN 16121) and slightly lower static loads for domestic use, reflecting Soviet-era design norms. [30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_GOST_standards)GOST standards were originally developed by the government of the Soviet Union as part of its national standardisation strategy. However, GOST 16371-2014 (the current general technical requirements standard) has been partially harmonised with EN 16122 methodology for export-market products.
    
6. **EN 1022 / seating stability** only enters the storage standard matrix when a storage unit has a seat function (storage ottoman, storage bench), at which point both the seating standard and the storage stability standard must be satisfied. [4](https://www.eurofins.com/toys-hardlines/resources/articles/ensuring-furniture-durability-strength-and-load-bearing-tests-explained/)Seating and storage stability is covered by EN 1022 (seating stability) and EN 16122 (storage).
    
7. **Cycle counts as accelerated life simulation**: [19](https://www.micomlab.com/micom-testing/bifma-x5-9/)ANSI/BIFMA X5.9 specifies acceptance levels ensuring reasonable safety and performance of storage units with an estimated product life of ten years based on a single-shift usage. EN 16121 Level 1 (50,000 door/drawer cycles) is similarly calibrated for ~10-year general commercial use; Level 2 (80,000 cycles) targets military/institutional severe use.

# G) Which tests are realistically “verifiable by calculation” vs “only by physical test”?

## Good candidates for calculation (engineering pre-checks)

1. **Shelf deflection under distributed load** (EN 16122 §6.1.4 with a deflection limit like **0.5% span**): classic beam/plate deflection calculation, then confirm physically. [4](https://catas.com/uploads/media/catas-tabella-comparativa-norme-mobili-eng-febbraio-2024.pdf?v3.4.3=)
2. **Top/worktop static load** (e.g., EN 14749 §5.3.10.3 750 N / 10×10 s): check bending + local crushing; then physical for joints/fasteners. [2](https://device.report/m/aea5cb569c08c0773501ced721fb369029b0d6a2a528cfd919b0bb4e4c5a6d47.pdf)
3. **Stability / tipping moment balance**:
    - EN 14749 uses a **potential energy** trigger and “shall not overturn” criterion (moment balance can pre-check). [2](https://device.report/m/aea5cb569c08c0773501ced721fb369029b0d6a2a528cfd919b0bb4e4c5a6d47.pdf)
    - EN 1022 (2005) explicitly allows **calculative method** with limitations (not for flexible/variable geometry). [6](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/bee825dc-3109-4b7b-87b0-72a211bf74be/en-1022-2005)
    - GOST 19882 stability is also fundamentally a tipping-moment problem (though the standard expects test setups). [12](https://normadocs.ru/gost_19882-91)

## Essentially physical-only (calculation won’t substitute for certification-grade confidence)

- **Impact/drop tests** (EN 16122 §6.4.2, shelf support impact, BIFMA out-stop impacts). [4](https://catas.com/uploads/media/catas-tabella-comparativa-norme-mobili-eng-febbraio-2024.pdf?v3.4.3=)
- **Durability cycling** (doors 20k–40k cycles; drawers 50k cycles; lock 5k cycles): wear, fastener loosening, and mechanism degradation dominate. [4](https://catas.com/uploads/media/catas-tabella-comparativa-norme-mobili-eng-febbraio-2024.pdf?v3.4.3=)
- **Dislodgement/retention of shelves/rails/wall-hung units**: friction, tolerances, and hardware behavior. [2](https://device.report/m/aea5cb569c08c0773501ced721fb369029b0d6a2a528cfd919b0bb4e4c5a6d47.pdf)

---

## Practical takeaway (how teams typically use this mapping)

- Use **calculation** to iterate design quickly (deflection, stress, tipping margin), then run **physical tests** for the “unknown unknowns” (fasteners, slides, hinges, slam/impact, manufacturing tolerances).
- For **CIS/EAEU trade**, make sure your test lab is aligning to the **TR CU 025/2012** framework and the **GOST method standards listed** in the applicable standard lists (not just “GOST in general”). [11](https://www.gostinfo.ru/trts/List/34)
