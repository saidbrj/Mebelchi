## SECTION 5 — THE PITCH NARRATIVE (One Paragraph)

The Uzbekistan furniture industry is a **$580M production market** served by **7,500 formally registered enterprises** — a number growing by nearly 1,000 per year. It is structurally fragmented, with 25–30% of output still made in informal household conditions, raw material costs eating 50–60% of revenue (mostly imported), and only 10% of the country's SMEs currently using any digital tools. The government is actively formalizing the sector — new regulations allow micro-workshops to hire up to 5 workers, furniture clusters are expanding across 8+ districts, and customs duties on 14 raw materials are being slashed. This creates a **window for a mobile-first SaaS tool** targeting the ~5,000 workshops in the "sweet spot" (5–50 employees, formally registered, hungry for order management and costing tools). At a conservative 15% penetration and $240/year, that is a **$180K ARR Year-5 SOM** — modest in absolute terms but on a market growing at 6%+ real annually, with a natural export path to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan (Uzbekistan's primary furniture export markets) where per-capita furniture spend is 6–8x higher.

## SECTION 1: BAZIS-MEBELSHIK (Russia) — The Dominant CIS Incumbent

### What It Is

[3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS is a modular system of furniture production and is described as "the only domestic computer-aided design system able to solve the problem of furniture enterprise integrated automation." [3](https://bazissoft.com/) The software tools cover all stages of furniture production, and the number of modules and configuration is specified individually for each enterprise.

### Module Architecture

[3](https://bazissoft.com/) The core of the system is BAZIS-Woodworker, a specialized graphics editor that allows designing 3D furniture models of any complexity. The ecosystem includes:

- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS-Cabinet, a graphics editor for fast model creation from standard templates; both modules work independently but are closely connected, forming a software complex for fast cabinet furniture design.
- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS-Cutting, a material cutting optimization module; the cutting area is the "heart" of furniture production and allows creating cutting charts that save materials and reduce cutting time.
- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS-Estimate calculates full costs automatically; BAZIS-Stock tracks materials and generates purchase lists, with data exportable to bookkeeping systems.
- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS-CNC creates control programs for saws and milling machines, with information imported from the product model and programs generated directly in CNC control codes, loadable by barcode scanning.
- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) BAZIS-Salon models any room and arranges furniture in real-time, allowing clients to see interior variants — useful for both salon managers and furniture designers.

### Pricing & Licensing

[4](https://allsoft.ru/software/vendors/bazis-centr/kompleks-programm-sistemy-bazis/) The software is listed on Allsoft (a major Russian software retailer) at a starting price from 96,000 RUB, which at current exchange rates (~90 RUB/USD as of mid-2025) equates to approximately **~$1,067 USD** for the base package. Full multi-module configurations scale significantly higher. **Pricing on request for non-Russian entities; estimated $800–$3,000+ USD for a full workshop setup based on module count.** [3](https://bazissoft.com/) Modules can be used in common installation mode (perpetual-style) or in Online mode with actual operating-time payment — suggesting a hybrid perpetual + metered/subscription model exists, though the online model is not prominently marketed internationally.

### Cloud / Mobile Offering

[5](https://planplace.online/en) PlanPlace, a partner platform, offers a "Business" tier that includes a Bazis subscription, indicating a nascent web/cloud wrapper exists via third-party integrators. However, **there is no native mobile app (iOS/Android) for BAZIS itself.** The cloud offering is server-hosted, not mobile-first.

### Uzbekistan Presence

There is an active Uzbek-language YouTube tutorial community for BAZIS: [8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tB00LYdgAqo)Uzbek-language tutorial videos (e.g., "Bazis mebelshik. 6-dars. chizmalar") exist on YouTube, with associated Telegram groups at t.me/bazis_uz — confirming grassroots local adoption but **no official Uzbek partner network publicly listed.**

### ✅ Does Well

- Most complete end-to-end furniture production suite in the CIS
- Deep CNC integration; industry-standard for Russian-speaking markets
- 30+ years of development; stable and trusted

### ❌ Not Right for a 4–15 Person Uzbek Workshop Because

- Entirely **Windows desktop-only**; no mobile or tablet access for floor workers
- Modular pricing means a full-featured setup costs $1,000–$3,000+ upfront — unaffordable for small shops
- [3](https://bazissoft.com/) With 30+ years of development, the codebase is legacy and the UX reflects it — high training overhead
- No official local support channel in Uzbekistan; Russian-language only (no Uzbek UI)
- International sanctions risk (Russian-origin software, payment friction post-2022)

### 🎯 Gap Left Open

A mobile-accessible, Uzbek/Russian bilingual tool with order management and cut-list generation at a $15–$40/month price point that doesn't require a dedicated CAD-trained employee.

### PRO100

[15](https://gidmaster.info/en/usefulness/soft/professional-software-for-working-with-furniture) PRO100 is a standalone furniture and interior design software with immediate stereoscopic visualization, distinguished by ease of maintenance, a clear interface, and the ability to build custom libraries. [14](https://www.pro100usa.com/) It covers cabinet makers (frame or frameless), kitchen design, closets, and more — producing 3D renderings with dimensioned floor plans, cut lists, job costing, and pricing, with optional panel optimizer or CNC package. [18](https://www.pro100usa.com/design-software-features) When you buy Pro100, you receive a USB dongle key; the program can be installed on as many computers as needed but requires the dongle to run. **Pricing:** Pricing on request; **estimated $300–$700 USD** for the base license (dongle included), based on community sources. No subscription option widely available. Desktop Windows only.

## SECTION 5: The Willingness-to-Pay Framework for Your Specific Buyer

### The "Egger Math" Test — Your Most Powerful Pricing Anchor

Your "Save a Board" feature is not a software feature. It is a **cost avoidance device**. Frame it with hard math:

text

```
One saved Egger sheet              = $40
Your software monthly cost         = $15
Net saving on first saved board    = $25
Sheets saved per month (avg.)      = 3–8
Monthly financial value delivered  = $120–$320
Your price / value ratio           = 4–21x ROI
```

At 4–21x demonstrable ROI, the conversation is not "is $15/month affordable?" — it is "why would I NOT pay $15/month?" This is the Van Westendorp **"so cheap it must be bad"** danger zone — if you price too low ($4–5), buyers assume the software can't possibly be sophisticated enough to deliver those savings.

## SECTION 6: Payment Friction & Currency Realities

This is the silent killer of international SaaS in Central Asia.

**The key barriers:**

1. **International card friction:** Many Uzbek small businesses operate with UZS-denominated accounts. Visa/Mastercard cross-border charges create friction and surprise costs
2. **Trust gap:** Paying an unknown app a monthly fee via card is culturally unfamiliar for workshop owners who still prefer cash-forward or invoice relationships
3. **Cancellation anxiety:** "What happens to my data if I stop paying?" is a bigger concern in markets where software data ownership is poorly understood

[30](https://upgrowth.in/saas-pricing-packaging-strategy-india-gtm/) Pricing in local currency builds trust with SME buyers and avoids surprise at checkout when currency conversion happens.

**Recommended payment architecture:**

- **Primary:** Payme / Click (local UZS billing) — eliminates all friction
- **Secondary:** Patreon-style card billing in USD for internationally-minded buyers
- **Annual option:** 20–30% discount for annual pre-pay (massive conversion lever in these markets)

### What this means for your pricing design

If you bill **$9–15 in USD via Stripe-like rails**, you risk losing customers due to:

- card declines / 3DS issues,
- lack of “business bank card that works internationally,”
- owner distrust of foreign recurring payments.

If you bill **in UZS** and accept **Click/Payme + bank transfer invoice**, your _effective willingness-to-pay_ goes up because payment becomes “normal.”

# 7) Defensible pricing recommendation (and the anchors that justify it)

## Recommendation: keep $9–15, but structure it as “local-normal,” not “Silicon Valley cheap”

I would ship with three tiers in **UZS**, optimized for payment psychology:

### Tiering (per workshop / organization, not per seat)

1. **Starter:** **99k–129k UZS/month** (~$8–11)
    - “paper belt” affordable
    - limit projects/jobs/month, or limit nesting runs/day
2. **Standard (most popular):** **149k–199k UZS/month** (~$12–17)
    - full nesting + grain/kerf + cutlist export
    - includes the “Save a Board” alerting mechanic
3. **Pro:** **249k–349k UZS/month** (~$21–29)
    - multi-material libraries, team workflows, CNC post outputs, quoting, inventory/offcuts, priority support

**Why these numbers are defensible (anchors):**

- They sit **below Bitrix24 Basic** (288k UZS/mo ≈ $24) so you’re not asking a small workshop to “buy a full CRM just to get nesting.” [3](https://www.bitrix24.uz/prices/)
- Your Standard tier sits around the **1C cloud “Basic” gravity well** (200k UZS/mo ≈ $16.7). That’s where Uzbek SMEs already accept recurring payments for core operations. [2](https://1c.uztelecom.uz/tarif_ru.pdf)
- They are consistent with the global “light optimizer” category (e.g., Cutlistor $12/mo). [13](https://www.cutlistor.com/)
- They respect the OECD-observed preference for **cost effectiveness / low commitment**, especially if you keep monthly billing, easy cancellation, and local payments. [8](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/digital-skills-for-private-sector-competitiveness-in-uzbekistan_6c54f447-en/full-report/component-7)

## The price-sensitivity threshold (where conversion likely drops sharply)

Based on the Uzbekistan anchors above, I’d treat these as the “cliffs”:

- **Cliff #1: ~~150k UZS/month (~~$12–13)**  
    This is where owners start asking “can I do this in Excel/Telegram?” If your onboarding doesn’t show material savings immediately, you’ll lose the long tail.
    
- **Cliff #2: ~~200k UZS/month (~~$16–17)**  
    This is a psychologically important threshold because it’s in the zone of **entry 1C cloud (200k)**—customers now compare you to accounting software and ask for similar seriousness (support, uptime, compliance, invoices). [2](https://1c.uztelecom.uz/tarif_ru.pdf)
    
- **Cliff #3: ~~288k UZS/month (~~$24)**  
    Now you’re in **Bitrix24 Basic** territory. Customers will ask “why not buy a full platform?” unless you’re delivering direct, measurable waste reduction or CNC output value. [3](https://www.bitrix24.uz/prices/)
    

So: **$9–15 is valid**, but **$15 must earn its keep** with obvious ROI (sheet savings, labor time saved, fewer mistakes, faster quoting).

---

## One tactical move that increases WTP without raising price: “ROI-denominated pricing”

Since your killer feature is literally: _“save 1 Egger sheet = $40,”_ bake that into messaging + UX:

- show a rolling counter: “saved X sheets / X UZS this month”
- let them set their local sheet costs
- convert every “dim tweak” suggestion into money saved

That reframes $9–15 from “software expense” into “small % of recovered waste.”
