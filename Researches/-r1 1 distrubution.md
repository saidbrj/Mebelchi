Your product is the **exact opposite** on every single axis. A CNC optimization SaaS requires:

- Operational proof before adoption
- Workflow disruption to implement
- Multiple stakeholders to convince
- Measurable ROI justification

An influencer can sell a $49 app because the cost of being wrong is lunch money. **They cannot sell a tool that touches a shop's production floor.**

## 5. How Shop Operators ACTUALLY Make Purchasing Decisions

This is where most tech founders fail — they project their own purchasing behavior onto their buyers. A 38-year-old shop owner running 4 CNC machines and 6 employees is not your typical SaaS-curious early adopter.

### The Cold Truths:

**① Trust is earned through proof, not personality** Shop operators are deeply skeptical of anything that hasn't been vetted by someone who runs a shop like theirs. An influencer with 400K followers who "loves woodworking" has zero credibility as an operational authority. A peer owner in their regional cabinet-making association who says "this cut my sheet waste by 18%" is gospel.

**② They buy solutions to bleeding problems, not features** They are not feature shopping. They are solving a specific, costly, recurring operational pain. Your pitch must land in the language of: material waste %, job turnaround hours, operator error rate — not "AI-powered optimization."

**③ The decision is rarely made alone** Even in a 5-person shop, the owner will consult the lead CNC operator, check with whoever does their books, and possibly call a buddy who runs a similar shop. Your sales process must arm the owner to sell internally — give them the ROI calculator, the comparison sheet, the case study.

**④ Demo or it didn't happen** Blue-collar B2B buyers need to see the product working on _their_ materials, _their_ machine configurations, _their_ file types. A 60-second reel means nothing. A 20-minute hands-on trial that saves them 2 sheets of MDF in a single session closes the deal.

**⑤ They are deeply risk-averse about operational tools** If your software glitches mid-job, it doesn't just cost them a subscription fee. It costs them material, machine time, labor, and potentially a client deadline. The perceived risk of adoption is enormous. No amount of influencer hype overcomes that — only **track records, testimonials from verified shops, and iron-clad support guarantees** do.

**⑥ Their information diet is nothing like yours** They're reading trade publications like _Cabinet Maker & FDM_, hanging out in niche Facebook groups for CNC operators, watching hyper-specific YouTube channels from machine manufacturers, and talking at regional trade shows like AWFS or IWF. **That is your media landscape.** Act accordingly.
