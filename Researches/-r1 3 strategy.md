## 30-day “Walk the Floor” distribution strategy (industrial zones) — designed to scale the unscalable

This is a **repeatable daily operating system** for selling a **$9–$15/month cutting-plan optimization subscription** _in person_ inside concentrated industrial parks. It’s built around: **(1) tight routing, (2) a 2‑minute demo, (3) same-day QR close, (4) relentless referral loops inside each zone**.

---

# 0) The rules of “walking into noisy workshops” (so you don’t get bounced)

### The environment constraint

On the shop floor, attention is expensive. Your opener must:

- Respect **safety + workflow**
- Get permission fast
- Land a **single, concrete outcome** (2-minute demo)
- Avoid “software pitch energy”

### Non-negotiables (field discipline)

- **PPE-ready** (glasses, boots, high-vis if needed). Don’t step over taped lanes.
- **Stand where they can see you**; don’t approach from behind running equipment.
- **Ask for the right person**: cutter/programmer/operator/lead, _or_ owner in small shops.
- **Never start with features**. Start with _waste + rework + time-to-plan_.

# 1) Your daily operational mechanism (the scalable loop)

Run this loop **every day** (including during the 30-day sprint):

### Daily scorecard (track on paper or notes app)

- Walk-ins attempted: **20**
- Meaningful contacts (spoke to cutter/programmer/lead): **8**
- 2-minute demos delivered: **5**
- Trials/paid scans (QR): **2**
- Instant referrals asked: **5**
- Neighbor introductions gained (same industrial park): **1**

### Daily cadence (works in most industrial zones)

**07:00–07:20** — Plan the route (“one park, one loop”)  
**07:20–07:40** — Pre-load demo (templates + a “their numbers” calculator)  
**08:00–10:30** — Walk-in block #1 (when crews are setting up jobs)  
**10:30–10:45** — Parking-lot notes + reset  
**11:00–12:30** — Walk-in block #2 (owners/estimators surface)  
**13:30–15:30** — Walk-in block #3 (less chaos, more conversation)  
**15:30–16:00** — Collect payments, schedule installs, line up tomorrow’s loop  
**16:00–16:15** — Update pipeline + tally metrics

### Territory scaling principle (this is the “distribution strategy”)

Industrial zones behave like **mini-networks**:

- people share operators
- people borrow material sources
- people talk at supply counters

So you “distribute” by **dominating one park at a time**:

1. land 2–3 paid users in the park
2. turn those into **neighbor intros**
3. repeat one park over

# 3) The exact first 30 seconds (noisy workshop opener)

You’re walking in; machines are loud; someone looks up annoyed.

### 30-second script (word-for-word)

> “Hey — I’ll be quick. I’m **[Name]**. I work with shops around here to **cut sheet waste and planning time**.  
> Who’s the person who **sets up the cut plans / nesting** — the operator or programmer?  
> I’ve got a **2-minute demo on my phone**. If it’s not obviously useful, I’m out of your way.”

If they point you to someone else:

> “Perfect — I’ll stand right here. I just need **two minutes** with the person who actually does the cut plan.”

If they say “What is it?” while walking away:

> “It’s a **quick optimizer**: you plug in parts + sheet size, and it kicks out a tighter plan. Most shops use it when they’re rushing and don’t want to burn material.”

### Why this works

- It’s permission-based (“I’ll be quick”)
- It respects roles (“who sets up cut plans”)
- It offers a clear micro-commitment (“2 minutes”)
- It has an easy exit (“I’m out of your way”)

# 4) The 2-minute cutting plan optimization demo (on a phone)

## The goal

In **<2 minutes**, they must see:

1. you can model _their reality_ (sheet size / kerf / grain / spacing)
2. the plan looks tighter (visual proof)
3. you translate it into dollars faster than they can object

## The “2:00 stopwatch” demo (script + timing)

### 0:00–0:15 — Set context + grab one number

> “What do you cut most on — **4x8, 5x10, or something else**?”

(Whatever they answer, choose the closest preset you already have.)

### 0:15–0:35 — Load a preset job (do not type if you can avoid it)

> “Cool — here’s a typical job: mixed parts, same sheet size. Watch the waste change.”

### 0:35–1:10 — Run optimization + show before/after visually

> “This is the ‘quick layout.’ Now I hit optimize…  
> See how it tightens the gaps and packs the parts? That’s the difference between ‘good enough’ and ‘best yield’ when you’re moving fast.”

(Zoom in briefly. Point at one obvious improvement: fewer “dead zones.”)

### 1:10–1:40 — Translate to dollars with _their_ rough costs

> “Ballpark — what’s a sheet cost you these days? **$___?**  
> Even saving **one sheet every [week/month]** pays for this many times over.”

(You’re not claiming a guaranteed %; you’re anchoring the math they already believe: material isn’t free.)

### 1:40–2:00 — Immediate close to QR

> “If you want it, it’s **$12/month** and you can cancel anytime.  
> Takes **45 seconds** — scan this QR, pay, and you’re live.”

# 5) The on-the-spot close (QR payment) — tight and mechanical

### 15-second close script (after demo)

> “Should we set you up on the **$12/month** plan so you can use it on the next job?  
> If you scan this, it’s a standard checkout page — you’ll get a receipt and you’re done.”

### Physical mechanic (so you can do it while standing)

1. Show your **laminated QR** (and also keep the QR as a lock-screen image on your phone)
2. They scan → hosted checkout opens
3. They pay
4. You immediately do:
    - confirm receipt email
    - write their name + shop + phone on your notepad
    - schedule: “I’ll swing back tomorrow / Thursday to make sure it’s working on a real job.”

### If they hesitate at the “scan”

> “Totally fair — this is just a link to checkout. No app install. If it’s easier, I can stand here while you do it, or I can step out and come back in two minutes.”

(You remove the awkwardness without losing the close.)

# 6) Top 3 objections + exact scripts to break them

These are the three you’ll hear most at $9–$15/month in industrial shops:

---

## Objection #1: “We already do nesting / we already have software.”

### What it really means

- status quo comfort
- fear you’ll slow them down
- “we don’t want another tool”

### Script (acknowledge → isolate → differentiate → micro-commitment)

> “Makes sense — most shops have _something_.  
> Quick question: are you **optimizing every job**, or only the ones that are tricky / expensive?  
> The reason guys keep this is it’s **faster than the full CAM flow** when you just need a tight plan _now_.  
> Give me **two minutes** — if it doesn’t beat what you’re doing on a mixed-part sheet, I’ll drop it.”

If they say their current tool is perfect:

> “Perfect is great. Then this will take **90 seconds** to prove I’m irrelevant.”

The key is you’re not arguing. You’re proposing a **test**.

---

## Objection #2: “Not now / we’re slammed.”

### What it really means

- they’re protecting throughput
- they assume you’re a time thief

### Script (respect → time-box → “stand here”)

> “I see it — I’m not here to blow up your schedule.  
> I can do this in **two minutes** standing right here.  
> If you don’t say ‘that’s useful’ by the time my timer hits zero, I’m gone.”

(Then actually run a timer. This is disarming.)

If they still push you off:

> “No problem. What’s better — **start of shift tomorrow** or **right after lunch**? I’ll be back for two minutes, same spot.”

You keep control by offering two concrete times.

---

## Objection #3: “I can’t buy / need approval / no card / we need a PO.”

### What it really means

- purchasing process friction
- fear of getting yelled at for a subscription
- they might still want it personally

### Script (separate _use_ from _procurement_ + get a foothold)

> “Totally normal. Two options:  
> **Option A:** you scan and put it on a card — it’s **$12**, basically a consumable.  
> **Option B:** if you truly need approval, tell me who approves it and I’ll walk to them **right now** while I’m here.  
> Either way, I want you using it on the next job so you can say ‘yes’ or ‘no’ based on reality.”

If they say “owner’s not here”:

> “Cool — I’ll write a one-line note for them: ‘This saves sheets / saves planning time — $12/mo — cancel anytime.’  
> I’ll come back **tomorrow at [time]** and you can just point me at them.”

(You turn “no” into a scheduled next step without leaving empty-handed.)

# 7) The 30-day “Walk the Floor” plan (day-by-day execution)

**Day 2 — Build assets**

---

## Phase 2 (Days 4–10): First park domination (get your first 10 pays)

**Every day**: one industrial park, full loop, then a second loop in a nearby park.

**Day 4** — First loop (10–15 walk-ins). Your only goal: **deliver demos**.  
**Day 5** — Same park again. Goal: **2 paid scans**.  
**Day 6** — Same park. Goal: **referrals** (“who else here cuts sheet?”).  
**Day 7** — New park. Repeat.  
**Day 8** — Return to Park #1 for follow-through + social proof.  
**Day 9** — Park #2 deeper coverage.  
**Day 10** — Install/support day (short visits to paid users):

- get one quote (“saved me a sheet on ___”) _if true_
- take a photo of a layout (if allowed)
- ask for 2 neighbor intros

Mechanism for scaling inside the park:

> “If this helps you, who’s the **closest shop** here that also cuts sheet? I’ll walk there next — mind texting me their bay number or pointing me?”

---

## Phase 3 (Days 11–20): Turn single closes into distribution

Now you operate like a distributor inside the zone.

**Daily requirement:** 1 paid + 1 neighbor introduction.

**Day 11–13** — Park #1 + Park #2 alternating

- You’re building “I’ve seen this already” recognition.

**Day 14** — “Referral sweep day”

- Visit every paid user for 3 minutes:
    - confirm they used it once
    - ask: “Who else should be using this?”
    - physically walk next door if possible

**Day 15–20** — Park #3 launch + repeat system

- Same play: demos → pays → intros → neighbor loop

---

## Phase 4 (Days 21–30): Standardize, then widen territory

At this point, you’re not “selling.” You’re running a machine.

**Day 21–23** — Park #3 domination (repeat visits create trust)  
**Day 24** — Objection audit + script tightening (rewrite what you heard verbatim)  
**Day 25–27** — Park #4 launch  
**Day 28** — “Purchasing friction day”

- revisit every “need approval” lead
- walk directly to the approver if on-site

**Day 29** — “Retention insurance day”

- quick check-ins so month-1 churn doesn’t erase your work:
    - “Show me the last job you ran it on.”
    - “What would make it faster to use?”

**Day 30** — Lock the playbook

- finalize your one-page SOP:
    - opener
    - 2-minute demo
    - close + QR
    - objection scripts
    - daily scorecard targets

## STRATEGIC FOUNDATION: The Scalable Framework for Unscalable Sales

The paradox you're solving is this: **direct sales doesn't scale in volume, but it scales in density.** You're not going to 1,000 cities. You're going to dominate 3 industrial zones so completely that every workshop talks to every other workshop. Word-of-mouth in a concentrated industrial zone is faster than any algorithm. Your salespeople are the distribution network. The QR code is the checkout counter.

**Core Operating Principle:** One rep. One zone. One 8-hour day. Minimum 25 door knocks. Maximum 3-minute conversations. Close on the floor or not at all.

## THE 30-DAY PHASE STRUCTURE

---

## ⚡ THE FIRST 30 SECONDS: Entry Script for a Noisy Workshop

**The situation:** You walk into a metal fabrication shop. Angle grinders. Sheet metal. The floor manager is yelling at someone. There is no reception desk.

**The cardinal rule:** Never ask permission to pitch. State a fact, then ask a question about _their_ operation.

---

### 🎙️ THE "WASTE HOOK" OPENING — 30-Second Script

> _(Walk directly toward the person who appears to be making decisions — not the first person who looks at you. Make eye contact. Raise your voice slightly to cut through noise. No handshake initiation — let them lead.)_

**YOU:**

> _"Hey — quick one. I work with sheet metal shops in this zone. Most of them are losing between 12 and 18 percent of material per sheet on manual cut planning. I can show you what your number looks like on my phone in about 90 seconds. You the one who decides how stock gets cut here?"_

---

**Why this works, line by line:**

|Line|Function|
|---|---|
|_"Quick one"_|Pre-empts the "I'm busy" shutdown before it happens|
|_"I work with shops in this zone"_|Social proof without a name drop yet. You're already established here|
|_"Losing 12–18%"_|Specific, credible number. Not "you could save money." Loss framing.|
|_"90 seconds"_|Removes the time objection before it's raised|
|_"You the one who decides"_|Qualification disguised as respect. If they say no, they immediately tell you who does|

**If they say:** _"I'm busy right now"_

> _"Completely fine — I'll show your guy. If it's useless, it cost you 90 seconds."_ _(Turn slightly toward a worker. The manager will almost always stop you.)_

## 📱 THE 2-MINUTE PHONE DEMONSTRATION

**Setup Requirements (non-negotiable before entering any shop):**

- Phone brightness at 100%
- App open to a pre-loaded sheet with your prospect's most common material size (2440×1220mm is standard)
- Sample "before" image ready (a photo of an actual offcut pile from a previous shop — get permission or use a stock industrial image you own)
- Demo mode, not live account

---

### ⏱️ The 2-Minute Demo — Beat-by-Beat

**[0:00–0:20] — The Anchor Visual** Show the "before" image (disorganized offcut pile or a manual cut sketch on paper).

> _"This is how most shops plan cuts. Paper, memory, or gut feeling. What does yours look like?"_

_(Let them answer. Don't interrupt. This is your only listening moment.)_

**[0:20–0:50] — Live Input** Type or tap their answer into the app. Enter: material size, part sizes needed, quantity.

> _"Okay — same job, your sizes, right now."_

_(Show the screen. Tilt it. Let them hold it if they reach for it — never stop them.)_

**[0:50–1:20] — The Reveal** App generates optimized cutting layout.

> _"It just found you [X]% more usable material from the same sheet. On 100 sheets a month, that's [Y] sheets you're not buying."_

_(Have a rough calculator pre-done: 100 sheets × material cost × waste % saved = dollar figure. Write it on the back of a card in front of them.)_

**[1:20–1:50] — The Conversion Bridge**

> _"This runs for nine dollars a month. Less than one offcut strip from your scrap pile."_

**[1:50–2:00] — The Close Setup**

> _"You want to lock it in now or you need the owner to see this?"_

_(Two options only. Never ask "what do you think?" That is a death sentence.)_

## 💳 THE ON-THE-SPOT QR CODE CLOSE

**Physical Setup:**

- Laminated QR card (business-card size, double-sided): one side = payment QR, other side = download QR
- QR links directly to a checkout page with monthly subscription at $9/$12/$15 tiers
- The tiers correspond to number of machines/operators, not features — simple upsell path

---

### 🔒 Close Sequence — The "Floor Sign-Up"

**Step 1 — Hand the card physically:**

> _"Here — this is your setup. Scan the bottom one first, that downloads the app. Takes 20 seconds."_

_(Watching someone scan eliminates the "I'll do it later" death zone.)_

**Step 2 — While app downloads, address the payment:**

> _"While that loads — top QR locks in your first month. It's nine dollars. No contract. Cancel from your phone any time."_

**Step 3 — Stay silent for 8 full seconds after saying the price.**

_(This is the hardest discipline in field sales. Most reps fill the silence. Don't. The silence is the close.)_

**Step 4 — If they scan and pay:**

> _"Done. You're in. I'll be back in this zone in two weeks — I'll check your first cut report with you. What's the best number to confirm?"_

**Step 5 — If they hesitate on payment:**

> _"Start with the app. Run it on your next job before you pay anything. If the layout doesn't beat what you're doing now, the QR goes in the bin."_

_(This removes the financial risk from the decision. Most will pay after the first successful run — and you follow up in 48 hours.)_

### OBJECTION #1: _"We already have a system / the owner handles this."_

**The Break:**

> _"That's exactly my point — if you don't have that number fast, the system isn't giving it to you. Show the owner this screen when he's in. I'll write the number we calculated on the back."_

_(Hand them the card with the dollar figure you calculated during the demo. The card does the owner meeting for you.)_

### OBJECTION #2: _"Nine dollars a month sounds cheap — what's the catch? What happens after the first month?"_

**What they actually mean:** _I've been burned by subscription traps before._

**The Break:**

> _"Good question — there's no annual lock-in, no escalating price, no call you have to make to cancel. It stays nine dollars unless you add a second machine, which bumps it to twelve. You cancel from the app settings in about four taps. Want me to show you where the cancel button is right now, before you sign up?"_

_(Showing them the cancel button before they subscribe is the highest-trust move in field sales. It almost always converts a skeptic.)_

### OBJECTION #3: _"My guys won't use an app. They're not phone people."_

**What they actually mean:** _I'm worried about implementation friction and resistance from my team._

**The Break:**

> _"I hear that a lot. Two things: first, the guy running the cuts doesn't need to use the app — you run it at the start of the job and hand him a printed layout or show him the screen. It's a planning tool, not a machine interface. Second — watch."_

_(Hand your phone to the most skeptical-looking person nearby.)_

> _"Show me how you'd enter a 600-by-400 piece on there."_

_(Almost everyone can navigate it in under 30 seconds. The live proof collapses the objection faster than any argument.)_
