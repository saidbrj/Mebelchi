# 09 — Q&A Playbook

**Version:** 1.0

Memorize these. They turn skepticism into investment, partnership, or customer commitment.

## Investor / judge questions

### Q1: "Why not just compete with Bazis directly?"

Bazis costs $2,000 and runs on Windows desktop. It's optimized for factory CAD designers with a multi-day learning curve. Our user is a small-shop mebelchi on a phone in the workshop. We're not competing — Bazis owns the high end, and we're solving for the 5,000 shops who can't afford it. They're paying Eman $2/m² today for raskroy services. We bring the same value at $15/month, mobile-first.

### Q2: "What's your moat? A factory could clone this in 6 months."

Three layers:
1. **Sticker-driven part traceability**: ERP-grade workflow at SaaS pricing. Once a shop's panels carry our QR codes, switching costs are high.
2. **Supplier data network**: 8+ suppliers paying for B2B listings means our materials catalog is the most accurate in the country. Competitors clone the algorithm but not the data.
3. **Telegram-native distribution**: $0 marketing growth, like Romchi. Distribution channel itself is a moat at this market depth.

### Q3: "Romchi is doing the same thing. How are you different?"

Romchi solved 1D cutting (aluminum profiles) for window manufacturers. We solve 2D cutting + 6-sided drilling for furniture manufacturers. The math is different, the supplier ecosystem is different, the user is different. We're not Romchi for furniture — we're Bazis for the masters Bazis ignores. Romchi's CTO is mentoring us; they're not entering this market.

### Q4: "Founder isn't a mebelchi. How will you build something they trust?"

Two answers:
1. The factory friend signed as advisor, vesting toward co-founder over 6 months. He's the credibility we lack on day one.
2. The first 30 days of the plan are field immersion — 30 shop visits — before any production code is written. We don't ship features we haven't seen the pain for.

### Q5: "Show me real customer money."

By day 22 of the 30-day plan: one supplier letter of intent for $5,000 annual contract. By month 6: 200 paying shops at $9/month = $1,800 MRR. By month 18: $25K MRR. Numbers track Romchi's actual ramp with 10× larger TAM.

### Q6: "Why now? Why not 2 years ago or 2 years from now?"

Three converging factors:
1. Telegram Mini Apps mature (2025) — distribution channel scales
2. Chinese CNC penetration (Excitech, KDT) crossed 50% of Uzbekistan small shops by 2024 — meaning the user has a CNC to feed
3. Bazis penetration plateaued at fabrika tier; small shop pain is now visible

Earlier: no CNCs to feed. Later: a factory-owner competitor (the $50K guy) ships first.

### Q7: "What if Eman builds this themselves?"

Eman makes money from small masters paying $2/m². Our software cannibalizes that revenue stream. Eman won't build the tool that destroys their factory revenue. The same dynamic protected Romchi from Akfa building a competing window app.

### Q8: "What's the path to $1B?"

Three layers:
- Layer 1: SaaS in Uzbekistan and Paper Belt (CIS + Turkey + Pakistan): $300M+ TAM at maturity
- Layer 2: Supplier data and materials marketplace: 2% take rate on facilitated supplier sales — Romchi's playbook applied to furniture
- Layer 3: Full ERP layer for mid-tier fabrikas: $99/month tier captures the Bazis upgrade gap

By year 7: $50M ARR is achievable with disciplined execution. $1B is a stretch but possible if marketplace and ERP layers compound.

## Operator (mebelchi) questions

### Q1: "Sizning ilovangiz Bazisdan nima farqi bor?" ("How is your app different from Bazis?")

Bazis $2,000, kompyuterda ishlaydi, o'qish uchun bir hafta kerak. Bizniki $15/oy, telefondan ishlatasiz, 10 daqiqada o'rganasiz. Bazis fabrika uchun. Biz sex uchun.

### Q2: "Stikerlar nima foyda beradi?" ("What's the benefit of stickers?")

Hozir ustangiz panelni qalam bilan belgilaydi. 10 ta paneldan 1 tasi noto'g'ri belgilanadi — qaytadan kesish, qaytadan kromka. Bizning stikerlarimiz QR-kod bilan, har bir mashinaga avtomatik aytadi nima qilish kerak. 10% gacha brak kamayadi.

### Q3: "DXF fayl mening stankimga to'g'ri keladimi?" ("Will your DXF work on my machine?")

Excitech, KDT, Nanxing — barchasiga to'g'ri keladi. Standart DXF formatda. Agar Homag bo'lsa, bizning fayldan o'tib NC Studio yoki GibLab-da kerakli formatga aylantirasiz, fabrika kabi.

### Q4: "Necha vaqtda o'rgataman?" ("How long to learn?")

Birinchi kuni — bitta oshxonani 10 daqiqada hisoblaysiz. Bir hafta ichida — to'liq usta darajasida.

### Q5: "Internet bo'lmasa ishlamaydi?" ("Doesn't work without internet?")

Asosiy funksiyalar oflayn ishlaydi. Faqat materiallar narxi va supplier ma'lumotlari yangilanish uchun internet kerak. Stansiyada Wi-Fi yetadi.

## Supplier (B2B) questions

### Q1: "What do I get for $500/month?"

- Branded listing in the materials catalog seen by 500+ small shops daily
- Real-time price sync if you provide an API
- Featured placement when a master selects your material category
- Monthly analytics: how many quotes included your products, which shops are buying
- Direct lead capture: shops that want bulk orders from you, with their contact info

### Q2: "How is this better than buying Telegram ads?"

Telegram ads are interruptive and disappear. Our listing is at the exact moment a master is preparing a quote — when they're choosing materials. That's a 100× higher intent context. Plus you get lead data, which Telegram ads don't provide.

### Q3: "How many shops are using this?"

Honest answer: as of now, [X] paid shops, [Y] in active trial. Founding pricing means you're in on the ground floor. By month 12 we project 1,000+ shops.

### Q4: "What if I want exclusivity in a material category?"

Premium tier ($1,000/month) includes exclusive sponsor placement in one material category. Limited to 5 categories total (LDSP, MDF, HDF, hardware-hinges, hardware-slides). First come, first served.

### Q5: "How do I pay? Click? Bank transfer?"

Both. Click or Payme for monthly auto-renewal. Annual prepay (10% discount) via bank transfer with proper VAT invoice.

## Team / hiring questions

### Q1: "Why should I leave my current job for your startup?"

Honest answer: because the alternative is watching another small-shop SaaS get built without us in the next 12 months and missing the window. We have $X cash runway, a defined V1, a paid mentor (Romchi CTO), and a proven adjacent playbook (Romchi). Equity is X% with 4-year vest. Salary during development: $Y. Salary post-MRR break-even: market rate.

### Q2: "What if the company fails in year 1?"

Most likely failure scenario: V1 ships, gets <100 paid customers, MRR plateaus under $3K. Outcome: shut down, return remaining capital to investors (if any). Your equity is worthless but you've shipped a real product, built it with the Romchi CTO's mentorship, and have a real reference. The downside is small. The upside is meaningful equity in a 10× TAM business with a proven playbook.

### Q3: "Will I be replaceable in a year?"

No. Module ownership is yours. You wrote it, you maintain it. As we scale, your role grows — first you own your module, then you hire to extend it, then you architect related modules. Equity vests over 4 years; we plan for you to be here that long.

## Hard challenges (the ones that hurt)

### Q1: "Your brother is doing something similar. Won't you compete?"

Honest answer: my brother's product is consumer-facing AI renovation. Mine is operator-facing production software. They are opposite ends of the same value chain. Eventually they might integrate (his consumer picks furniture → my shop produces it). For now, hard separation. He's not entering my market; I'm not entering his.

Internal note: this answer is also true. Stop worrying about brother dynamic and ship.

### Q2: "Romchi failed at marketplace. Why won't you?"

Romchi's marketplace was a consumer-facing aluminum marketplace. We're not building a marketplace — we're building a production tool that, by V3, optionally includes a marketplace layer for standardized cabinet orders. Different scope, different sequencing. Marketplace is not in V1, V2, or V3 in any blocking way.

### Q3: "You're a designer, not an engineer. How can you architect this?"

I'm orchestrating, not coding the core algorithms. I have a senior backend engineer (Saidislom), a frontend engineer (brother), and a senior CTO mentor (Romchi). My role is to lock conventions, define contracts, design the product surface, and ensure no module ships without passing the Definition of Done. I write Python where needed (DXF generator, label generator) but I don't pretend to be the core engine builder.

### Q4: "Why are you doing this instead of working with Davron at Romchi?"

We're brothers, not co-founders. Different operating styles, different products. We help each other through mentorship and shared infrastructure (CTO mentor relationship), but we run separate companies. This is healthier than trying to merge.

### Q5: "What if I (mebelchi) try it and it doesn't work for my specific cabinet type?"

V1 supports kitchens only. If your work is mostly wardrobes or office furniture, V1 is not for you — wait for V2 (~6 months). I won't sell you V1 and pretend it covers everything. If kitchens are 50%+ of your volume, V1 saves you time today.

## The closer (always end on this)

When someone is wavering at the end of a pitch, the line is:

> "Look at the receipts. Romchi has $25K MRR in windows — proven playbook. Our TAM is 10× larger. The factory friend has already committed. The Romchi CTO is mentoring us. The brand and architecture are locked. The first supplier is in. The competitor with $50K is still hiring his CTO. We have the receipts and the head start. The only question is whether you want to be inside this or watching from outside."

Make eye contact. Don't say anything else. Let them decide.
